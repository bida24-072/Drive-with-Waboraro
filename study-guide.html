Theory Test Study Guide

Your complete revision companion — road signs, rules, and test-day tips.

1. Road Signs — Warning Signs

Warning signs are yellow diamonds. They alert you to hazards ahead. Slow down and be ready to react.

SignMeaningHow to React
⚠️ Pedestrian CrossingPedestrians crossing aheadSlow down, be ready to stop
⚠️ Sharp BendRoad curves sharplyReduce speed before the bend
⚠️ Slippery RoadSurface may be slipperyIncrease following distance
⚠️ Narrow BridgeBridge narrower than roadSlow down, check oncoming traffic
⚠️ School ZoneChildren may be crossingReduce speed to 30 km/h

2. Road Signs — Regulatory Signs

Regulatory signs tell you what you must or must not do. Ignoring them is an offence.

SignMeaningWhat to Do
🛑 StopComplete stop requiredStop fully, check, then proceed
⛔ No EntryDo not enterFind another route
🔴 Speed LimitMaximum speedDo not exceed the number shown
⚠️ YieldGive wayLet other traffic go first
🚫 No ParkingParking prohibitedDo not stop or park
🚫 No OvertakingOvertaking prohibitedStay in lane until sign ends

3. Right of Way Rules

At a Four-Way Stop

  1. First vehicle to arrive and stop has right of way
  2. If two arrive at the same time — yield to the vehicle on your right
  3. Never assume — make eye contact before proceeding

At a Roundabout

  1. Give way to traffic already in the roundabout
  2. Signal left when exiting
  3. Do not change lanes inside the roundabout

At an Unmarked Intersection

  1. Yield to vehicles on your right
  2. Yield to vehicles already in the intersection
  3. Yield to pedestrians crossing

4. Speed Limits in Botswana

Road TypeSpeed Limit
Residential / Built-up areas60 km/h
Rural roads80 km/h
Main highways120 km/h
School zones30 km/h
Near hospitals40 km/h

Test Tip: Always watch for signs that override the default limit. If a sign says 40 km/h in a residential area, that limit applies — not the general 60 km/h.

5. Vehicle Controls

Foot Controls

  • Accelerator (right pedal): Increases speed
  • Brake (middle pedal): Slows or stops the vehicle
  • Clutch (left pedal — manual only): Used when changing gears

Hand Controls

  • Gear lever: Selects forward, reverse, or neutral
  • Handbrake: Keeps the car stationary when parked
  • Indicators: Signal before turning or changing lanes (30m in advance)
  • Wipers: Clear rain from windshield
  • Hazard lights: Alert other drivers of danger

6. Safety & Emergency Procedures

If Your Brakes Fail

  1. Pump the brake pedal rapidly
  2. Change to a lower gear to slow down
  3. Use the handbrake gently — do not yank it
  4. Look for a safe place to stop (grass, gravel)

If You Have a Tyre Blowout

  1. Grip the steering wheel firmly
  2. Do NOT brake suddenly
  3. Ease off the accelerator
  4. Let the car slow naturally
  5. Pull off the road when safe

At an Accident Scene

  1. Stop if involved — it is the law
  2. Turn on hazard lights
  3. Check for injuries
  4. Call emergency services (997 police, 998 ambulance)
  5. Exchange details with other drivers
  6. Do not admit fault — let insurers decide

7. Test Day Tips

What to Bring

  • Valid Omang (national ID) or passport
  • Proof of application / booking reference
  • A pen (in case you need to sign documents)
  • Prescription glasses if you wear them for driving

The Night Before

  • Get a full night's sleep — do not cram
  • Review the road signs one more time
  • Eat a proper breakfast — hunger affects focus
  • Plan your route to the test centre

During the Test

  • Read every question twice before answering
  • Answer the easy questions first — do not get stuck
  • If unsure, eliminate obviously wrong answers
  • Trust your first instinct — do not second-guess
  • Do not rush — you have time

Remember: This is just a test. If you do not pass the first time, it is not the end of the world. Many of my students passed on their second attempt. Stay calm, study consistently, and you will get there.
